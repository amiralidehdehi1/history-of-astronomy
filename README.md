# A Historical and Mathematical Study of the Transition from Geocentrism to Heliocentrism

## Overview

This project examines the historical development of astronomical models from geocentrism to heliocentrism, focusing on the scientific ideas and developments that shaped this transition.

It explores the contributions of major figures in the history of astronomy and investigates how observations, mathematical reasoning, and physical theories gradually changed our understanding of planetary motion and the structure of the Solar System.

Beyond its historical perspective, the project aims to examine the mathematical models and physical principles underlying these astronomical systems, with particular attention to their explanatory and predictive capabilities.

## Research Scope

The study is organized around three main areas:

### 1. Historical Perspective
- The origins and development of the geocentric model.
- The astronomical systems of Aristotle and Ptolemy.
- The emergence of the heliocentric model through the work of Nicolaus Copernicus.
- The contributions of Tycho Brahe, Johannes Kepler, and Galileo Galilei.
- The development of classical mechanics and its role in explaining planetary motion.

### 2. Mathematical Analysis
- The geometrical foundations of ancient astronomical models.
- Circular motion and the use of epicycles.
- Mathematical descriptions of planetary motion.
- Kepler's laws of planetary motion.
- A comparison of the mathematical structures of geocentric and heliocentric models.

### 3. Physical Analysis
- The physical assumptions underlying early astronomical models.
- The problem of planetary motion and retrograde motion.
- The development of classical mechanics.
- Newton's laws of motion and universal gravitation.
- The physical explanation of planetary orbits.

## Research Objectives

The main objective of this project is to understand the scientific transition from geocentrism to heliocentrism by examining the historical, mathematical, and physical arguments behind it.

Particular attention is given to the relationship between astronomical observations, mathematical models, and physical explanations, as well as to how changes in these areas contributed to the development of modern astronomy.

## References

This project draws on historical astronomical texts, scientific publications, academic books, and other relevant scholarly sources.
